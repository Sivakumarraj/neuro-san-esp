01 · Context, action, outcome
What is optimised, and against what?

Context — a question with a checkable answer. The search prescribes one network for all of them; the router in 04 prescribes per question.

Action — a network: its shape, its instructions, and a model per agent.

Outcomes — accuracy, tokens, dollars.

fitness = accuracy
  − 0.06 · min(tokens / 300000, 1)
  − 0.02 · (agents / 9)
A fixed formula over measured or predicted outcomes — never learned.
01 · The genome
A candidate is a neuro-san network.

An agent_network_definition in neuro-san's own HOCON, plus a model per agent. It renders straight to a registry and runs as it is.

The model is part of the genome hash, so a score measured on one model is never mistaken for the same network on another.

Order is not cosmetic. neuro-san enters through the first agent listed. Written alphabetically, flat_pair put Arithmetic in front and scored 0.00; written as designed, 0.82.

02 · The surrogate
What does the Predictor actually predict?

Asked in review by a co-author of the ESP paper. The first version was one model trained on the already-scalarised fitness.

Now: one regressor per outcome — accuracy, tokens. Agent count is counted, not predicted. Fitness is derived.

The split found a defect one number had hidden. Accuracy ranks: +0.63 against a null of −0.07. Token cost ranks backwards: −0.53, in 20 of 20 seeds — left out of the ranking, published, not patched.

03 · What did not hold up
Does it hold on questions it never chose on?

The first search: 12 networks, 17 questions, on a free tier. Split the questions in half, 200 times: the winner on one half ranked first on the other in 0% of splits. Mean rank: 7.2 of 12.

Seventeen questions cannot rank networks.

Every claim since is held to a held-out test.

03 · Out of sample
Two results that went the other way.

Held-out bank, 24 questions: the designer's shape 23, the evolved network 21.

First paid run, 100 judge questions: the designer 68, the designer with a stronger router 68 — at 2.1× the cost per question.

"Strong model on the router" did not survive a held-out test.

04 · Where the errors are
Penalty arithmetic, not retrieval.

16 of 140 questions charge several incidents at their contracts' penalty rates. They hold 30 of the designer's 52 wrong runs, and 14 of 2cc4's 19.

At most 2 of 60 wrong runs look like a missed document. The confident misses found both contracts and subtracted the rates: 4375 − 3875 = 500, never multiplied by the hours.

Found by running it
What made good networks look bad.

Timeouts and blown recursion caps were scored as wrong answers. A rate limiter slept inside the event loop. A cost term clipped every network to one penalty.

"429" inside incident INC-4429 tripped the quota guard. Replies listing 0 to 20 held 90 of 200 answers, until grading read only the number a reply commits to.

Every token and dollar figure before 10 October was counted twice. The logs are kept; the reader halves them.

Before any money
An audit, as a reviewer would.

Every command rerun from a clean install. Every committed number reproduced.

Four statements stronger than their evidence were corrected where they stood — a 90% with no baseline among them: a random network scores 85.5%.

05 · Production
Can it run unattended?

Event-invoked. neuro-san's own server fires the optimizer on a cron as user_id: system, no client attached. Verified on neuro-san 0.6.95.

The model never chooses or spends. RunWake ignores its arguments; ReportFinding can only draft. Silent unless something improved.

Built to be cut short: preflight, lease, atomic state after every candidate, a cache keyed by genome hash, and a dollar cap on every paid run.

Said before a reviewer finds it
Where this is not ESP.

No learned Prescriptor. Prescription is seven operators plus Pareto selection — nearer to LEAF (GECCO 2019), with agents where LEAF had layers.

The paid result does not test the Predictor. Offline it picks the best of three unseen networks 62% of the time — level with picking the one with the most agents.
